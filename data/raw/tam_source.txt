TAM/SAM/SOM Analysis Source: Residential EV Charging Subscription Market

Total Addressable Market (TAM):
The global home EV charging equipment market was valued at approximately $6.1 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $18-22 billion by 2030, reflecting a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of roughly 20-22%. In the US alone, there were an estimated 3.4 million EVs on the road in 2024, with EV sales expected to represent 30-35% of new vehicle sales by 2030. Each EV household represents a potential home-charging customer, and with average US household EV charger spend (hardware plus installation) around $1,200-$1,800, this implies a US home charging TAM in the range of $4-6 billion annually by 2030 at current adoption trajectories, before accounting for recurring subscription/software revenue layered on top of hardware.

Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM):
Narrowing to single-family and townhome-owning EV households in states with supportive right-to-charge legislation and active utility rebate programs (California, New York, Colorado, Texas, Washington, Massachusetts) reduces the addressable base to an estimated 1.1-1.4 million EV-owning households as of 2024, growing to a projected 4-5 million households by 2030 as EV adoption accelerates in these states. At an estimated average annual subscription value of $300-$480 (bundling hardware amortization, smart-charging software, and maintenance), this represents a SAM of roughly $330-$670 million annually within the initial 6-8 target states, growing toward $1.5-2 billion by 2030 as both EV adoption and state coverage expand.

Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM):
Given the fragmented competitive landscape (no single home-charging subscription provider currently holds more than an estimated 8-10% market share nationally) and typical early-stage market penetration rates of 1-3% within the first 3 years of operation for a well-executed regional launch, a realistic 3-year SOM target for a new entrant focused on 2-3 initial states would be approximately $8-15 million in annual recurring revenue, assuming successful capture of 15,000-25,000 subscribing households at the estimated per-household annual subscription value. This assumes a customer acquisition cost (CAC) in the $150-$300 range, consistent with reported CAC benchmarks for consumer subscription hardware-plus-service businesses, and a subscriber base concentrated in the underserved multi-unit-dwelling and renter segments identified in prior competitive analysis, where incumbent single-family-home-focused competitors have weaker existing penetration.

Market Growth Drivers:
EV sales growth, continued (though potentially reduced) government incentives, declining battery and charger hardware costs (approximately 8% annual decline), and increasing time-of-use utility rate programs that reward smart, off-peak charging all support sustained TAM/SAM expansion through 2030. The primary risk to these projections is a slowdown in EV adoption rates due to macroeconomic conditions, reduced subsidy availability, or slower-than-expected public charging infrastructure buildout, which could compress the growth curve on the upper end of current 2030 projections.
